A challenge enters the Reference when at least three of the four qualifying institutions name it. Institutions are counted, not items. The Reference is fixed for an epoch; a refresh check runs each 1 October, and any change to the institution set, the threshold, the merge protocol or an institution's edition opens a new epoch. No trend is drawn across an epoch boundary.

Limits

  1. Disclosure base. At publication, nine of the register's eleven records rest on documents published by public allocation programmes. In the documents the pilot examined, governments state quantities in money or aggregate exaflops, and corporations state them in press releases, which are not among the classes that pass the gate.
  2. Regional concentration. Three European-authored institutions and the United Nations. No Asian, African, or Latin American institutional list qualified: the non-English lists found are national research-priority lists, a different genre, and failed on scope. The Reference measures agreement among these four institutions, and two of them survey perceived risk. Adverse outcomes of AI is a published challenge on that basis, and AI-safety compute is counted under it. The definition is published so that the regional concentration can be tested at each refresh. The programmes whose assessment ends the pilot are European, United States, and Australian public allocation programmes and their regional distribution does not offset this concentration.
  3. Energy supply. Two of the four institutions name it. The threshold excludes it. Energy commitments are recorded, assigned, and held. They enter the table at the first refresh at which a third institution names energy.
  4. Privacy and data protection. The run table has no data-protection candidate. The nearest is erosion of human rights, a sub-threshold candidate. A block of research in the National Science Foundation's allocation export (differential privacy, federated learning, unlearning, de-identification) is therefore assigned but unpublished, or logged as matching no candidate.
  5. Programme purpose. 441 of the pilot's passages mapped to no candidate and 174 stated no purpose, against eleven records. Under the assignment rule, a purpose that names an AI method and a domain only as its application is logged as matching no candidate, so part of the 441 reflects the rule's strictness.
  6. Asymmetry of publication. One actor's two records represent a programme for which the search found no published award list. Another actor's one record represents a programme that has published 847 awards, and that record supplies eight of the nineteen document–challenge pairs. Counts are floors of disclosure, not of activity.
  7. Non-quantification. A record's presence carries no scale information beyond its band.
  8. Attribution edge. At publication the edge holds one record and one event.
  9. Maintenance. Open-ended intake, the epoch discipline of the Challenge List Reference, and evidence archiving are recurring work the Observatory undertakes for the register.
  10. Band D. The class gate excludes press releases, blogs, and keynotes. A corporate or philanthropic pledge made only in such a document cannot enter, and at publication the register holds no Band D record.
  11. Discovery circularity. The external intake was model-assisted: research models found the instruments. What the models did not find is not in the register, so its coverage reflects their reach.
